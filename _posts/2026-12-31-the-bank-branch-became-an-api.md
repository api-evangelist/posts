*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act one: reprice.*

This is the story where the good side of APIs is easiest to see, which is why I want to be careful with it.

Bank branches are disappearing. In the UK the number of bank, building society and credit union branches fell from 10,410 in 2019 to 6,870 in 2024, a 34 percent drop. In the US, closures have outnumbered openings every year since 2010, with more than 4,000 closing in 2020 alone. An American Bankers Association survey found 54 percent of customers mainly bank through a mobile app and only 9 percent prefer a branch.

The Richmond Fed says the main driver is how profitable deposits are, which depends on interest rates and on technology. So apps are part of the cause, not all of it.

## The good side

In Kenya, mobile money skipped the branch entirely. Tavneet Suri and William Jack, writing in Science in 2016, estimated that access to M-Pesa lifted about 2 percent of Kenyan households, roughly 194,000, out of extreme poverty, with the biggest gains for households headed by women. Other researchers have challenged their method, but it remains the most cited evidence that a payments platform on a phone can do real good.

## The other side

The people a branch closure hurts most are the ones least likely to switch to an app: older customers, rural towns and people without reliable connectivity. And later in this series, I will look at loan apps that run on the same mobile money rails and use the borrower's phone against them.

## What the APIs show

On APIs.io, [Plaid](https://apis.io/providers/plaid/) scores exemplar and is rated agent-native. [Revolut](https://apis.io/providers/revolut/) publishes 51 APIs and scores exemplar. Then it falls off. [Chime](https://apis.io/providers/chime/) scores thin, [Nubank](https://apis.io/providers/nubank/) scores minimal, and [Venmo](https://apis.io/providers/venmo/) publishes no APIs at all. Safaricom, which runs M-Pesa, is not in the catalog yet.

The neobanks that benefited most from closing the branch split sharply on whether they open up to the people and developers they serve.

## Why it matters

Open banking is the best example I know of an API built to give power back to the customer. Consented, scoped access to your own financial data is the legitimate version of what a lot of apps simply take. The branch became an API. Whether that API works for the customer depends on who designed it and who it answers to.
