*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act one: reprice.*

Delivery apps typically charge restaurants a commission of 15 to 30 percent per order, before delivery fees and payment processing. Wharton and NYU Stern both describe that same fee stack. Restaurants run on thin margins to begin with, so a cut that size changes what a delivery order is worth to the business that cooked it.

I want to be careful with numbers here. A lot of the scarier figures you will see, like all-in costs of 30 to 40 percent or margins of 3 to 5 percent, come from companies that sell restaurants their own ordering software. They may be right, but they are not neutral.

The most interesting evidence is about what happened when cities tried to fix it. A 2024 paper in Information Systems Research looked at cities that capped delivery commissions. When the caps lowered what platforms could charge independent restaurants, the platforms recommended those independents less, promoted chains more, and raised fees on customers. The cost did not go away. It moved.

## What the APIs show

On APIs.io, [DoorDash](https://apis.io/providers/doordash/) publishes 49 APIs and scores exemplar. [Grubhub](https://apis.io/providers/grubhub/) publishes 10 and scores strong. These are good APIs. They let a restaurant push its menu, take orders and dispatch drivers.

But look at whose business they serve. The APIs make it easy for a restaurant to plug into the platform. They do not hand the restaurant the thing that matters most, which is a durable relationship with the person who ordered. The customer belongs to the app.

There is a counterweight in the catalog. [Thanx](https://apis.io/providers/thanx/), a loyalty and engagement platform that restaurants run for themselves, also scores exemplar. The same quality of API can be used to rent a restaurant's customers back to it, or to help the restaurant keep them.

## Why it matters

A good API is not the same as a fair one. The question to ask of any platform API is simple: after the integration, who owns the customer record? For most restaurants on delivery apps, the honest answer is not the restaurant.
