*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act one: reprice.*

Text messaging used to be one of the most profitable things a phone company sold. In 2013 global SMS revenue was about $120 billion. Informa forecast it would fall to $96.7 billion by 2018 as people moved to messaging apps. A 2014 industry tally counted $386 billion in revenue lost to Skype, WhatsApp and similar services. Carriers went from selling messages and minutes to selling data, the so-called dumb pipe.

These are mostly forecasts, and carriers had every reason to describe competition as a loss. But nobody disputes the direction.

## The fight moved to business messaging

What is interesting now is the second round. Consumers left SMS years ago. Businesses still use it for receipts, alerts and one-time passcodes. Juniper Research forecasts carriers losing more than $3 billion in business SMS revenue to messaging apps over five years, with app-based business messages growing from 100 billion in 2023 to 375 billion in 2028. Juniper also blames fraud, which has made SMS less trusted, and carriers raising their prices.

## What the APIs show

On APIs.io, [WhatsApp](https://apis.io/providers/whatsapp/) publishes 74 APIs and scores exemplar. [Twilio](https://apis.io/providers/twilio/) publishes 153 and scores exemplar too.

Here is the part I find telling. Messaging moved from the carrier to an app that people use for free. Then the app opened a business API and started selling messaging back to companies. The value did not disappear, it moved from a regulated carrier to a platform owned by Meta, which now decides the price of reaching a customer on it.

## Why it matters

This one is less about harm to people and more about who ends up as the gatekeeper. Carriers were never popular, but they were regulated utilities. A messaging app's business API is a private toll booth on how a company reaches its customers. Developers should know which one they are building on.
