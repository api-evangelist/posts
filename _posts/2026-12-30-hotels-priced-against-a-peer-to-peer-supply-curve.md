*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act one: reprice.*

Hospitality has the best academic evidence of any sector in this series. Georgios Zervas, Davide Proserpio and John Byers studied Airbnb's arrival in Texas over a decade and published the results in the Journal of Marketing Research in 2017. In Austin, where Airbnb supply was highest, they estimated a causal hit to hotel revenue of 8 to 10 percent.

What I like about the paper is that it explains how. Hotels did not mainly lose bookings. They lost pricing power. During big demand peaks like SXSW, hotels used to raise rates sharply because rooms were scarce. Airbnb supply can grow quickly when demand spikes, so the scarcity went away and so did the price spike. Budget hotels and hotels that do not depend on business travelers were hit hardest.

## The second squeeze

Short-term rentals are only half the story. The other half is the online travel agency. Hotels now pay commissions that commonly run 15 to 25 percent to the platforms that send them guests, and those platforms own the search, the reviews and often the customer relationship.

## What the APIs show

On APIs.io, [Booking.com](https://apis.io/providers/booking-com/) publishes 49 APIs and scores strong. [Airbnb](https://apis.io/providers/airbnb/) publishes 11 and scores developing. [Expedia Group](https://apis.io/providers/expedia-group/) publishes 15 and scores thin.

The connectivity APIs these platforms offer are how a hotel pushes rates and availability into their marketplaces. They are also how the commission is collected. The integration is built so the property can sell through the platform easily. Getting the guest relationship back out is much harder.

## Why it matters

This is the reprice pattern at its clearest. The app did not just compete with hotels, it changed the supply curve they priced against, and then the booking platforms put themselves in the middle of the rooms that were left. The research is from Texas and from an earlier period, so I would not stretch the 8 to 10 percent number to every market today. The mechanism is what carries over.

Sources: [Zervas, Proserpio and Byers (BU)](https://people.bu.edu/zg/publications/airbnb.pdf), [open.bu.edu](https://open.bu.edu/handle/2144/40311).
