*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act three: automate.*

The Bureau of Labor Statistics expects cashier jobs to shrink even while retail sales grow. Its 2024 to 2034 projections put the decline at about 10 percent, roughly 313,000 jobs, and it names the reasons directly: self-service checkout and online shopping. In the previous ten-year projection, cashiers had the biggest expected job loss in raw numbers of any occupation.

Cashier work turns over constantly, so there are still around 540,000 openings a year. That is why this feels slow. Nobody gets a letter saying their job was automated. The jobs just are not refilled.

## What the APIs show

The catalog has the far end of this already. On APIs.io, [VenHub](https://apis.io/providers/autonomous-solutions/) describes itself as robotic retail: smart stores that run around the clock with no staff. It publishes 25 APIs and scores thin. Those APIs cover products, orders, payments, loyalty points and guest checkout. That is the whole job of a cashier and a stock clerk expressed as endpoints.

Amazon is licensing its Just Walk Out checkout technology to other retailers. And the retail systems behind ordinary stores, like [Microsoft Dynamics 365](https://apis.io/providers/microsoft-dynamics-365/), which publishes 28 APIs and scores strong, are where point-of-sale, inventory and staffing are wired together.

## Why it matters

Cashier jobs have long been the first job for millions of people, and a fallback job for millions more. They teach showing up, handling money and dealing with the public. When a store becomes a set of APIs, the transaction gets faster and the store gets cheaper to run. What goes missing is the job that let someone get started.

This is a projection, not a measured outcome, and the forecasts have been wrong before. But the direction is set, and it runs straight through the integration layer.
