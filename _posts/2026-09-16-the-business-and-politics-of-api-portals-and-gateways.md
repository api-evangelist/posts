I was talking with a young Sales Development Representative (SDR) at a startup this week about companies I have knowledge about. I love the eagerness of sales, but I very much enjoy the enthusiasm of youthful sales. They are very useful when it comes to trying to map, understand, and penetrate the sprawling surface area of enterprise customers. An API portal always provides a wonderful opportunity to try and understand and penetrate a large enterprise, but I find that people unfamiliar with the business and politics of the API space miss a lot of opportunities when it comes to reading the tea leaves available within a portal.

First, the lack of a public API portal will tell you a lot about the confidence of an enterprise when it comes to making digital resources available to partners, as well as making APIs available in their own mobile applications. It will shape the decisions they make when it comes to using the APIs of the 3rd-party SaaS solutions they have already purchased, and frame their reliance on artificial intelligence vendors when it comes to making the shift towards agentic. If teams don't have the experience to make digital resources available publicly or tap into the digital resources of the public services they already use, you will need to take a more traditional top-down procurement-focused approach to penetrating an organization and often need to rely on the core tech stack they've adopted (ie. Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, AWS) to get in the door.

Next, I'd spend some time considering how many portals there are. Spend some time Googling, probing, and crawling where other APIs exist, other teams that have published APIs to support their needs. You also might find other data portals, or other types of portals that showcase open-source, AI, or other API-related or adjacent efforts. The number of portals will help you frame the business and politics of the enterprise, how much of a mandate exists for everyone to use the same developer portal front door, or are partner teams up to other things, and are there other lines of business and politics framing how digital resources are being made available publicly. Claude, or other agentic solution can help you with this probing and reverse engineering of the surface area of enterprise organizations. Then you can reverse engineer some of the people behind these efforts via contact, support, social media, blogs, job postings, and other types of communication channels where you'll find interface mentions.

Finally, probing the gateway source of API calls helps define the shape beyond just portals. Often times the API portals and API gateways are aligned for an organization, but this isn't always the case. The business and politics of the enterprise will often centralize, but can also aggregate in a federated way around the gateway as well. Especially when it comes to powering partner integrations and 1st-party applications. This is a line you will want to be careful at when publishing your findings publicly, as most enterprises will consider the gateway behind integrations, as well as the APIs behind applications and integrations to be private, even if they operate via public DNS. The added gateway awareness combined with single or multiple portal awareness will provide the foundation for your understanding of how an enterprise approaches their business integrations.

When assessing API operations from the outside-in, it is easy to get too focused on the technology of APIs, but understanding that the business and politics of an organization will gather at the portal and gateway layers is critical to understanding who you need to be engaging with when it comes to sales, support, and other types of outreach. Also, the absence of a portal, and lack of information regarding API gateways, will help you understand what you are up against when it comes to getting people's attention. Enterprises without a portal rarely also pay attention to external signals when it comes to APIs, open-source, and other market and community activities. If you are an API service provider hustling in the space, the maturity of the portal and gateway, which I measure across various [Kin Score](https://apis.io/rating/) facets and metrics, will define the opportunity to engage with an enterprise, strike up conversations, and get their attention. The signals present via API portal or portals will frame how short or long your sales and outreach lifecycles will be.
