*Part of [The Dark Side of the API Economy](https://apievangelist.com/2026/12/17/the-dark-side-of-the-api-economy/). Act three: automate.*

This one is about us, the people who build software.

The best data I have seen on AI and jobs comes from Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar and Ruyu Chen at Stanford, in a paper called "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" They used payroll records from ADP, the payroll company. Workers aged 22 to 25 in the jobs most exposed to AI saw employment fall 13 to 16 percent relative to other groups, depending on the version of the paper. Older workers in the same jobs held steady or kept growing. The drop showed up where AI does the task, not where it helps someone do it.

So the damage is not to whole professions. It is to the first rung of the ladder.

## Software is one of those jobs

The numbers for junior developers point the same way but are all over the place. Reports of the Stanford AI Index 2026 put the drop for developers aged 22 to 25 at around 20 percent from the 2024 peak. Indeed's data reportedly shows junior software postings down about a third between early 2024 and early 2026. Other figures you will see online claim far more, and I would not repeat them without a source.

There are also real confounders. Tech companies over-hired and then corrected after 2022, and interest rates rose. How the government classifies the work matters too. The Bureau of Labor Statistics shows "computer programmers" falling about 27 percent from 2023 to 2025, while "software developers" barely moved.

## Freelancers saw it first

A study from Washington University in St. Louis and NYU looked at Upwork after ChatGPT came out. Freelance writers saw 2 percent fewer jobs and 5.2 percent lower monthly earnings, and image work followed the same pattern. High earners were hit just as hard. Upwork points out that larger contracts grew and that freelancers with AI skills earn more. Both things can be true: the more routine the work, the more exposed it is. On APIs.io, [Upwork](https://apis.io/providers/upwork/) publishes 7 APIs and scores thin.

## Why it matters

Every senior engineer I know learned on junior work: the bug fixes, the small integrations, the first API client. If companies use coding agents to skip hiring for that work, the savings show up this year, and the shortage of people who know how systems actually behave shows up in five or six. Nobody's API documentation is going to fix that. The people who decide whether to hire juniors will.
