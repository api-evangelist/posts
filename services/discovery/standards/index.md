      <div class="mb-2" style="max-width: 46rem;">
        <p class="mb-3">I have watched more interoperability die from a lack of boring agreements than from any hard technical problem. Two teams, two formats, two ways of saying the same date &mdash; and suddenly integration is a project instead of a given. Standards are the boring agreements that make the interesting work possible.</p>
        <p class="mb-3">I help you identify which standards actually apply to your operations &mdash; OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, JSON Schema, Arazzo, and the domain-specific ones your industry already leans on &mdash; and where you may need to establish your own. Then I help you adopt them in a way that sticks, so they show up in your interfaces and your pipelines rather than living in a slide deck.</p>
        <p class="mb-3">Standards are how you buy interoperability once instead of renegotiating it on every project. I have been tracking the API standards landscape since before most of these specifications had version numbers, and I bring that history to bear on what is worth your attention and what is noise.</p>

        <h2 class="fs-6 fw-semibold mt-4 mb-2">What you walk away with</h2>
        <ul class="text-muted">
          <li>A shortlist of the standards that matter for your specific operations</li>
          <li>A plan for adopting them consistently across teams and interfaces</li>
          <li>Interoperability treated as a default, not a per-project negotiation</li>
        </ul>
