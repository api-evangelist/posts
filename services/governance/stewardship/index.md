      <div class="mb-2" style="max-width: 46rem;">
        <p class="mb-3">The ruleset format that most of the industry governs APIs with today lives <em>inside</em> one company's linter, as a config file with no separate specification and, since the acquisition, very little investment. I have watched this pattern break my heart for sixteen years &mdash; API Blueprint, RAML, Swagger, one good open thing after another absorbed and left to stall. Governance is too foundational to leave sitting there.</p>
        <p class="mb-3">So the work is to lift the ruleset out and make it a <strong>first-class, documented specification</strong> the whole community owns &mdash; a real spec with a strong JSON Schema, education around it, and a vendor-neutral home alongside OpenAPI and Arazzo. When I talked with a longtime maintainer of the original linter, this is exactly what he pointed to: the canonical implementation, clearly specified, is what unlocks a healthy tooling ecosystem &mdash; the way Swagger did for OpenAPI. Without it, everything is buried in one implementation and everyone else is reverse-engineering a black box.</p>
        <p class="mb-3">There is a right way and a wrong way to fork. The wrong way is one strong personality declaring "here is my approach" and walking off &mdash; a fork that optimizes for one company's edge case and never invites anyone in. The right way is community-led: get the people who built and depend on the thing back around the table, put the spec somewhere neutral, and steward it in the open. That is what I am doing, working with others from the standards community, and it is what I can help you do &mdash; whether you are a provider who wants a seat at that table or an organization that needs its governance to rest on a standard that will outlive any single vendor.</p>
        <p class="mb-3">I do this in the open under <a href="https://apicommons.org">API Commons</a>: the ruleset format is being specified as <a href="https://github.com/api-commons/spotlight-spec">Spotlight Spec</a> with its JSON Schema on SchemaStore, a community linter fork, and a suite of tools around it. Open tooling is how a standard earns trust; stewardship is how it stays healthy.</p>

        <h2 class="fs-6 fw-semibold mt-4 mb-2">What you walk away with</h2>
        <ul class="text-muted">
          <li>A governance foundation that rests on an open, documented specification &mdash; not one vendor's config file</li>
          <li>A seat and a voice in a community-led effort to keep the ruleset format neutral</li>
          <li>Provenance and portability: rulesets that outlive whatever tool you run them in</li>
          <li>A path to contribute the spec into a neutral standards home alongside OpenAPI and Arazzo</li>
        </ul>
